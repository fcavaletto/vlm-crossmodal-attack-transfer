# Checkpoint 3: why does PGD-3 pick damaging coordinates? Exploratory plan and development-only pilot

Federico Cavaletto · drafted 2026-11-09 · branch `checkpoint-2.3/exploratory-paths`

**Status: EXPLORATORY. Development data only.** This plan was committed before any pilot code ran. It fixes the pilot's conditions, outcomes, and the rules for reading them. The pilot is exploratory: its readings decide whether a confirmatory protocol is worth writing, and nothing here is confirmatory. The Checkpoint 2.4 confirmation split (`study2-trackB-confirm-v1`) is **not used**, not even arm b. The reserved H\* / H-ΔL samples are not used. No `confirm-v2` split is generated by this checkpoint; that needs a report to the research lead first.

## 1. What is confirmed, and what this asks

**Confirmed (Checkpoint 2.4, held-out, pre-registered).** On Qwen3-VL-2B in bf16 at ε = 1/255:
- the official PGD-3 image is FGSM restricted to about 30% of its RGB channel coordinates;
- that subset does 2.8× the margin damage of a random subset of the same size and signs (+6.50 logits, CI 6.08–6.92);
- sparsity explains about 3% of the PGD-versus-FGSM gap;
- the subset ranked highest by clean NLL-gradient magnitude does worse than random.

**Not established.** *Why* PGD's subset is damaging. We also do not claim that the clean gradient carries no useful information. Only two functions of it were tested, magnitude ranking and the first-order dot-product prediction, and both misled. Other functions of the clean gradient were not tested.

**Question.** PGD-3 keeps a coordinate exactly when the gradient signs at x, x₁ and x₂ all agree, where x₁ and x₂ are the next two points on its path. Is PGD's advantage explained by **gradient sign consistency across nearby inputs**, a property of the local gradient field? Or does it depend on the particular points PGD visits, or on something else entirely?

**Working model (a hypothesis, not a finding).** g(x) = s(x) + n(x). Here s is a component that stays stable across the ε-ball and is relevant to damage, and n is a rough component that decorrelates over much smaller distances. A sign vote over several gradients enriches sign(s). Under this model, the vote should work at points chosen independently of the attack.

## 2. Prior work to respect before any novelty claim

| Idea | Established by | Implication for us |
|---|---|---|
| Averaging gradients over input noise gives cleaner attributions | Smilkov et al. 2017 (SmoothGrad) | "Averaged gradient beats raw gradient for saliency" is not new. What could be new is whether it repairs the *inverted* matched-count ranking we confirmed. |
| Expectation over transformation; obfuscated and noisy gradients | Athalye, Engstrom, Ilyas & Kwok, ICML 2018 (EOT); Athalye, Carlini & Wagner, ICML 2018 | Attacking through an expectation over random inputs is standard. A consensus attack is a cousin of EOT, not a new principle. |
| Random step before FGSM | Tramèr et al., ICLR 2018 (R+FGSM) | Gradients at random nearby points are known to help single-step attacks. |
| Neighborhood gradient variance for transfer | Wang & He, CVPR 2021 (Variance Tuning, VMI-FGSM; arXiv:2103.15571) | Sampling N gradients in a β·ε neighborhood is an established attack component. Our sampling scheme is close to theirs. |
| Spatially smoothing the gradient | Dong et al., CVPR 2019 (TI-FGSM) | Spatial smoothing changes which regions are attacked. Our patch-matched control is needed so that regional selection is not mistaken for coordinate-level selection. |
| Gradients of deep networks behave like noise | Balduzzi et al., ICML 2017 (shattered gradients) | The "rough component" idea has a precedent, there in an analysis at initialization. |
| Majority vote over gradient signs | Bernstein et al., ICLR 2019 (signSGD with majority vote) | Sign voting as a denoiser is known in optimization. |
| Sign-based black-box attacks | Al-Dujaili & O'Reilly, ICLR 2020 (SignHunter) | Gradient signs carry most of the attack-relevant information. |
| Sign agreement thresholds for one-shot FGSM | Ciosek et al. 2026 (arXiv:2605.14868, gradient prediction) | FGSM tolerates gradient noise until coordinate-wise sign agreement drops below a threshold. This is reported second-hand, and we have not read the paper's own analysis yet. |
| Sparse attacks choose coordinates | Papernot et al. 2016 (JSMA); Croce & Hein, ICCV 2019; Croce et al., AAAI 2022 (Sparse-RS) | Coordinate selection matters. Those methods search for few pixels. We study an attack that already exists, at matched count, at the 8-bit floor. |

The search on 2026-11-09 was limited, not systematic. Shattered gradients, Variance Tuning and the 2026 gradient-prediction paper were checked against search results. The other entries come from the existing reading list, or from memory, and must be verified before a manuscript cites them.

**What could be new, if the pilot and a confirmation support it.** Three things:
1. a matched-count, sign-matched test that **explains** an iterative attack's coordinate selection with an attack-independent property of the gradient field;
2. controls that separate that property from bf16 numerics, 8-bit grid effects, and spatial clustering;
3. a VLM setting.

Neighborhood averaging as such is **not** new.

## 3. Pilot data and budget

- **Scenes.** The 16 development scenes `s16` (`development_v4`, arm a; the same deterministic subset used for fp32-vision measurements in Checkpoint 2.3). These are the only scenes with stored fp32-vision PGD iterates, which the precision control needs.
- **Source context.** `neutral` only, to keep the pilot small. Every image is scored in both contexts, so cross-context transfer is still measured.
- **Reused, never recomputed.** Stored from 2.3 and 2.3b:
  - the clean gradients, both NLL and margin;
  - the PGD-3 int8 iterates (replayed with the frozen update, `masks.replay_pgd`) and the fp32-vision FGSM and PGD-3 iterates;
  - the official scores of FGSM, PGD-3, top-|∇| and the 8 random masks.
  The smoke re-scores PGD-3 and random seed 0 on one scene and requires a bit-exact match before the pilot may run.
- **New gradients per scene:** 14 NLL gradients in the neutral context:
  - 8 at dense random points (R);
  - 4 at quantized random points (Q);
  - 2 at sub-level jitter points (J).
- **New images scored per scene:** 14 masks or attacks × 2 contexts = 28 official forward passes.
- **Runtime estimate.** About 5–6 min per scene from earlier timings (about 18 s per gradient), so about 1.5 h for 16 scenes. The smoke measures it. Cap: 120 min, applied per scene. If the smoke projects more than the cap, the scene count is not cut silently; the plan records the change first.
- **Memory.** EMP-1 with M1 thresholds, unchanged. Same operations as the 2.4 run (bf16 only, no fp32 tower; 2.4 peak 8.54 GiB).

## 4. Conditions

Notation:
- g₀ is the clean NLL gradient in the source context.
- r_F is the rounded FGSM perturbation, with support S_F.
- k is the size of PGD-3's rounded support. It is matched per scene.

Every **mask** applies FGSM's own values r_F on k coordinates of S_F. That matches PGD-3's coordinate count and signs exactly, as in 2.3b and 2.4. Coordinate ties are broken by a seeded uniform key. All seeds come from `scene_seed("checkpoint-3-pilot|…", scene_id)`.

**Gradient sampling points** (independent of the attack path; each point is clamped to [0, 1]):

| Set | Point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **R** (8) | x + (ε/4)·σ, with σ a random ±1 sign pattern on every coordinate | The same step size as PGD, in random directions. |
| **Q** (4) | x + ε·σ on a random 1/16 of coordinates, 0 elsewhere | Valid 8-bit images, with the same L2 norm as R. Each change is two bf16 units in the normalized tensor, so it always survives the bf16 cast. This tests whether the effect depends on off-grid points or on bf16 erasure. |
| **J** (2) | x + (ε/16)·σ on every coordinate | Sub-level jitter. Its 8-bit image equals x, and most of it is erased by the bf16 cast (2.3 q0). This tests whether near-zero, numerically scaled moves already change the gradient. |

**Masks and attacks scored** (neutral source; official scorer; both contexts):

| Name | Selection over S_F (k coordinates) | Gradient evaluations |
|---|---|---|
| `vote_R1`, `vote_R2`, `vote_R4`, `vote_R8` | rank by agreement count a = #{g ∈ {g₀, g_R1..g_RM} : sign g = sign r_F}, highest first | 1 + M |
| `smooth_R2`, `smooth_R8` | rank by sign(r_F)·mean(g₀, g_R1..g_RM), highest first (aligned SmoothGrad) | 1 + M |
| `vote_Q4` | vote over g₀ and 4 Q gradients | 5 |
| `vote_J2` | vote over g₀ and 2 J gradients | 3 |
| `vote_P32` | vote over g₀ and the fp32-vision clean-gradient sign (stored) | 1 bf16 + 1 fp32 |
| `pgd_patch_s0`, `pgd_patch_s1` | random coordinates of S_F with **the same count per 16 × 16 pixel patch** as PGD's mask | 3 |
| `voteR8_patch` | the same patch-matched shuffle, applied to `vote_R8` | 9 |
| `sfgsm_R2`, `sfgsm_R8` | **attack, not a mask:** a dense full-ε step along sign(mean(g₀, g_R1..g_RM)). Its signs can differ from FGSM's. | 3, 9 |

Reused references: FGSM (1 gradient), PGD-3 (3), top-|∇| (1), and random (mean of 8 seeds, 1).

**Fair comparison by gradient count.** PGD-3 uses 3 gradient evaluations: x and the two path points. The equal-budget comparisons are `vote_R2`, `smooth_R2`, `vote_J2` and `sfgsm_R2`, at 3 gradients each. `vote_R2` is the direct path-free analogue of PGD-3's rule: 3-way sign agreement at x and two random points, instead of two path points. The R1–R8 series traces how damage depends on budget.

## 5. Outcomes

For each image, D is the source-context margin drop (clean margin minus perturbed margin, official scorer). D_target is the same quantity in the helpful context.

- **Selection.** Sel(X) = D_X − D_rand.
- **Recovered fraction.** R(X) = mean_s Sel(X) / mean_s Sel(PGD-3), a ratio of means over the 16 scenes, with a percentile bootstrap CI over scenes (9,999 draws, seed 20261110).
- **Overlap with PGD's mask.** |A ∩ P| / k, against chance k / |S_F|. Also the Spearman correlation of per-token mask density (32 × 32-pixel regions, the merged visual-token footprint) between A and P.
- **Referent region.** Mask density inside the referent object's grid cell, divided by density over the whole image.
- **First-order prediction.** −g_M·r, as in 2.4.
- **Gradient geometry.** cos(g, g₀) and sign agreement with g₀, for each of the R, Q and J gradients.
- **Transfer.** Sel and R computed on D_target.

## 6. Readings, fixed now

These rules are exploratory. They decide what to say, and whether a confirmation is worth proposing. They are not tests. Here R means R(`vote_R8`), the strongest pure consensus mask, and R₃ means R(`vote_R2`), the equal-budget one.

| Reading | Rule |
|---|---|
| **A. Consensus explains PGD's selection** | R ≥ 0.8 with CI lower bound ≥ 0.5, **and** the overlap with PGD's mask is well above chance (mean overlap minus chance ≥ 0.1), **and** R(`vote_J2`) ≤ 0.3 (tiny numerical moves do not reproduce it). |
| **B. Consensus predicts part of the selection** | The CI of R excludes 0, but A is not met. |
| **C. Consensus fails** | The CI of R includes 0, or R < 0.2. |

**Qualifiers that change the interpretation, not the reading:**
- **Spatial.** Suppose `pgd_patch` keeps most of PGD's selection (R ≥ 0.7). Then PGD's advantage is largely *regional* at 16-pixel scale, and consensus may be finding regions rather than coordinates. Compare `voteR8_patch` with `vote_R8` the same way.
- **Quantization and bf16.** Suppose `vote_Q4` ≈ `vote_R4`, both within 0.2 in R. Then off-grid points and bf16 erasure are not needed. If Q is much weaker, the R-point effect may depend on where the bf16 cast rounds.
- **Precision.** `vote_P32` measures what a *precision change alone* (bf16 versus fp32 vision, same input) contributes as a second vote. The overlap of the fp32-vision PGD mask with the bf16 PGD mask, against chance, shows whether PGD's selected set survives a change of numerics.
- **Budget.** Report R₃. If R₃ ≈ 1 at equal budget, consensus needs no path. If only R8 reaches it, consensus needs more gradients than PGD does.
- **Exploit.** Report D of `sfgsm_R2` and `sfgsm_R8` against PGD-3 and FGSM. This is descriptive and is not a claim of a better attack.

Spatial association, meaning token-density correlation and referent-region enrichment, is reported as *association only*. It is not evidence about any internal mechanism.

## 7. Integrity

- **Code guards.** The pilot reads only development fixtures and the stored 2.3 and 2.3b outputs, and refuses any path under `fixtures/trackB_confirm_v1`. It writes only to `docs/checkpoint-3-mechanism/pilot[_smoke]/`.

## 9. Implementation notes (dated, before any pilot outcome)

- **2026-11-09, smoke attempt 1.** It stopped on a guard before writing any record: `pgd_patch_s0` had k − 7 coordinates. PGD-3's rounded support contains a few zero-gradient half-level ties outside S_F (§3 of the 2.4 report), and the patch shuffle draws only from S_F. The fix pads patch-matched masks to k with seeded random coordinates of S_F (at most 7 of about 170,000), so every mask keeps exactly PGD-3's count. Before stopping, the smoke had already passed its bit-exact re-score checks. Its files are kept in `pilot_smoke_attempt1/`. No outcome was computed or seen.
